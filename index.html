The Kegillion field guide

Start reading ↓
01

What is Kegillion?

Kegillion brings campus-themed questions into a short browser game. This website introduces our developing edition, its rules, and the college-life ideas behind the question library. Start with the game card above if you want to try the current build, or read the guide before choosing a mode. You do not need to create an account or install an application. Kegillion is still in development: the artwork, answer coverage, and balance may change as the game takes shape.

Kegillion rules
02

A campus is more than a party

The starting point for Kegillion is American campus life: shared spaces, study breaks, dining choices, sports, music, and the everyday decisions that make a place feel familiar. A question can be an invitation to notice something ordinary from a new angle. Campus culture varies between institutions and between students at the same institution, so no single answer represents everyone. Kegillion treats its fictional campus as a setting for curiosity, rather than a claim about how all students live. You can enjoy the themes without being a university student, following college sports, or knowing every piece of American slang.

Explore the campus guide
03

Two modes, two ways to think

In Kegillion Rare mode, lower valid scores are better. You are looking for an accepted answer that the editors rated as less familiar. In Popular mode, higher scores are better: choose something the answer table treats as more familiar. These are two directions through the same question library, not two different measures of intelligence. The selected mode is worth checking before you begin. Kegillion scores describe an editorial model, not a live poll of students. A surprising answer is not automatically accepted, and an ordinary answer is not automatically wrong. Read the prompt carefully and match the kind of response it requests.

Kegillion rules
04

Daily rhythm or open-ended practice

Kegillion Daily selects seven questions for your topic using the date in US Eastern time. Each question has a twenty-five-second timer. Changing scoring modes does not create a different daily question set. Practice is the more relaxed route: it offers another selection and lets you choose whether to use a timer. If you want to learn how the accepted answers work, begin there. The first completed daily result for a topic is stored on this browser; later attempts are practice replays. Kegillion does not currently provide a public leaderboard or an account that carries your results between devices.

05

Choose a topic you actually enjoy

The Kegillion library is organised around campus life, food and snacks, games and sports, music and screen culture, and schools and the wider world. Mixed play offers a broader selection. Pick a familiar topic when learning the controls, then branch out when you want a different challenge. The current library contains fifteen hundred prompts, with at least six accepted examples per prompt. That size does not mean every reasonable answer has been covered or every entry has received independent fact checking. Kegillion is a work in progress, and useful feedback includes the exact prompt, your answer, and why you think it fits.

06

Meet your campus character

Choose a student character before a session. Your Kegillion avatar changes how you appear in the campus scene, not which questions you receive or how your answer is scored. Mia, the campus host, introduces prompts and reacts to your results. The character artwork shown here comes from the developing game and is illustrative artwork, not a screenshot of a verified play session. A character can make a short round feel more personal, but the important feedback remains readable text. Kegillion should explain the outcome even when you do not recognise a gesture or prefer less movement on screen.

07

Read the result, not just the number

After an accepted answer, Kegillion shows the matched entry and its explanation alongside the score. Take a moment to compare your interpretation with the prompt. A podium crowd illustrates the editorial familiarity band; it does not count real people who submitted your answer. Rare mode rewards a valid zero, while a skipped or timed-out question is marked separately as a miss. Popular mode aims in the other direction. Kegillion keeps this distinction because a low number alone cannot tell you whether a response was successful. The rules page gives the complete bands and explains the penalty for an unfinished question.

Kegillion rules
08

When an answer is missing

An unlisted response in Kegillion may still be a reasonable response in real life. The game checks an edited answer table rather than understanding every possible phrase. Try a clear name, check spelling, and avoid adding an explanation inside the answer field. If the entry remains unrecognised, you can try another response while time remains or skip the question. Kegillion supports a local feedback draft so that missing entries can be described precisely. Downloading that draft does not send it to anyone. Keep personal details out of feedback; the prompt and proposed answer are usually enough to explain the issue.

09

Make a better study break

Try Kegillion alone first, then compare reasoning with a friend after your round. Ask why one accepted answer felt obvious while another did not. That discussion can be more interesting than comparing totals, particularly when players come from different backgrounds. Do not treat an editorial familiarity score as a ranking of cultures, schools, or people. Kegillion is a quiz experience, not an academic assessment or a drinking activity. For a calmer session, use untimed practice and read the explanations. For a short challenge, try the daily set and leave the answer review until you have completed your own attempt.

10

Where the collection goes next

Kegillion begins with an American campus setting. Future ideas include European student life, Japanese and Korean campus experiences, Australian university culture, and South American student communities. These are planned directions, not playable regional editions. Each deserves locally informed questions, appropriate language, and careful review rather than a quick change of flags. The current Kegillion collection therefore shows one game instead of filling the page with unavailable play buttons. As new experiences become real, this shared navigation can make them easy to discover. There is no announced release date, and the present library should not be mistaken for comprehensive regional coverage.

11

Your progress stays close to home

Kegillion uses browser storage for local progress when storage is available. Private browsing, clearing site data, or moving to another device can remove or separate that progress. There is no account recovery service in this edition. Sharing starts only when you choose the share action, and the available sharing options depend on your device. If sharing is unavailable, the game offers result text for copying. Before relying on a Kegillion session across devices, remember that local saving is different from cloud synchronisation. Read the privacy page for the current storage and analytics position, and use Contact to report a problem.

12

Start Kegillion with one good question

Open the Kegillion game page, read the development notice, and choose the available build when you are ready. The how-to-play guide stays close at hand, and the campus culture guide explains how to approach the themes without relying on stereotypes. If the embedded view is uncomfortable on your screen, use the separate game link. Kegillion is being built around a simple promise: clear choices, readable feedback, and an enjoyable reason to think about campus life. The website will grow alongside the game, with honest availability labels and useful guides rather than claims about features that have not been delivered.
