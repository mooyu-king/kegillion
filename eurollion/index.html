THE IDEAS BEHIND THE COLLECTION

Campus culture is more than a postcard.

Eurollion begins with a simple creative question: what would a campus quiz look like if it made room for the different ways people study, meet, and find their place? The sections below explain the direction of the future collection. They are an introduction to its themes, not a finished game manual or a list of accepted answers.

01

Everyday campus life

A campus begins with ordinary places: the room where a seminar happens, the table where a group meets, and the noticeboard where someone finds a society. Eurollion takes those small encounters as its starting point. Rather than making every question about famous buildings, the proposed collection explores the vocabulary and decisions that make student life recognisable. A library visit, a shared kitchen, or a journey between classes can offer a useful conversation without assuming that every student has the same routine.

The important distinction is between a familiar setting and a universal claim. A courtyard in this artwork belongs to our imagined university, not to every university in Europe. When a future question concerns a particular institution or country, that context needs to be named. This approach leaves room for different schedules, living arrangements, ages, and routes into higher education.

02

Languages, names and local context

A word that looks familiar can carry a different meaning in another setting. For the future European question collection, clear wording matters as much as an interesting topic. A prompt should tell you whether it is asking for a place, an object, a local term, or a broader idea. This page introduces the English-language presentation; it does not promise a multilingual answer engine that has not been built.

Our editorial direction is to keep local names meaningful rather than treating every expression as an interchangeable translation. Accepted spellings and alternative names will need deliberate review when the question bank is developed. Until then, the examples here are discussion starters, not an answer list. If you suggest a phrase, include the language, setting, and intended meaning so that the context can travel with the word.

03

Student communities and shared spaces

A student community can form around music, a sport, a reading group, a volunteering project, or a shared interest that never becomes an official club. Eurollion is interested in those meeting points. The planned subject is campus culture in a broad sense, with room for quiet routines and creative interests alongside larger social occasions. Party knowledge should not be the only way to recognise yourself in a campus game.

The courtyard is a visual meeting place for this idea. Benches, covered paths, and a noticeboard suggest different ways to join in, while the open space leaves the scene welcoming. It is a fictional environment, so there is no claim that a particular university provides these facilities. Later content can distinguish a local tradition from a general activity instead of turning one institution’s custom into a rule for everyone.

04

Food, cafés and the pause between classes

Food offers a friendly starting point for conversations, but it also needs careful boundaries. A future prompt might focus on a named dish, a kitchen object, or a situation with a clearly stated setting. It should not imply that every student in a country eats the same meal, drinks the same drink, or follows the same daily schedule. Personal preference, access, and dietary needs are part of the picture.

For the visual direction, warm colours suggest a comfortable break between activities. That atmosphere is not a promise about a real campus café or a particular menu. We want the eventual questions to reward attention and curiosity, not a narrow stereotype of what a European student is supposed to enjoy. Food-related suggestions are most useful when they identify the specific place or tradition being discussed.

05

Getting around a university town

The bicycles in the artwork give the courtyard a lived-in feeling. They are part of a fictional scene, not evidence that all European campuses are cycling campuses. Student journeys can involve walking, public transport, accessible transport, or living close to a teaching building. A useful question must identify its setting before asking players to make assumptions about how someone gets to class.

The town beyond the courtyard also matters to the concept. Eurollion is not limited to an isolated university island: the imagined setting leaves space for streets, public places, and the everyday relationship between studying and living somewhere. This page is an introduction to that artistic direction. It does not provide travel planning, campus accessibility advice, or recommendations about a real university’s transport services.

06

Traditions with a place and a story

A tradition becomes more interesting when its origin and context are explained. The intended editorial approach is to name the community involved, describe what the practice means there, and avoid suggesting that every institution shares it. That makes a question more precise and gives its explanation something useful to say after an answer. The future game still needs its own researched and reviewed content.

You do not need prior knowledge of a particular country to explore this page. Read the scene as an invitation to notice differences, not as a test of whether you belong. If you propose a tradition for the collection, a university page or another direct source is more useful than a general claim about an entire continent. The goal is an understandable question with a clear scope, rather than a surprising fact without enough context.

07

A European collection, with room for differences

Europe is the regional direction of Eurollion, not a claim that there is one shared campus experience. The planned collection can explore different countries and communities while keeping the setting of each question visible. We have not published a country coverage list, and this introduction should not be read as a promise that every region is already represented in a completed question bank.

The name combines a European association with the playful rhythm of the wider campus collection. The subtitle, European Campus Trivia, explains the intended subject more directly. The current page supplies original artwork, a character introduction, and an editorial direction. A playable version will require separate development, including its questions, input handling, instructions, and a clear explanation of any scoring rules.

BEFORE YOU EXPLORE

Eurollion questions, answered.

Can I play Eurollion now?

Not yet. This is the Eurollion introduction and artwork page. The European game, its questions, and its scoring system will be developed separately. There is no hidden start button or playable iframe on this page. You can explore the campus, meet Lea, and save this page to your browser favorites.

Is this the same game as Kegillion?

Eurollion is a separate European campus concept within this website’s collection. The existing Kegillion page contains the developing US campus game. We are not presenting that American question bank as European content. The collection links below let you visit each page with its current availability clearly labelled.

Who is Lea?

Lea is our fictional adult exchange-student host for Eurollion. Her cardigan, notebook, and canvas bag belong to the character design, not to a national uniform. She introduces the visual setting; she is not a real student, a representative of a university, or an interactive chatbot.

Is the background a real European university?

No. It is an original imagined courtyard with stone buildings, covered walkways, plants, and bicycles. The illustration combines an artistic atmosphere with space for a future interface. It is not a photograph, a reconstruction, or a claim about the facilities at any specific university.

Will the game use rare answers or popular answers?

The final European rules are not available yet. The existing Kegillion game offers Rare and Popular modes, but this page does not promise that Eurollion already supports either system. Its eventual instructions will describe the implemented rules and explain the basis of the scores.

Does saving Eurollion sign me up for updates?

No. The heart button saves the page in this browser’s Favorites collection. It does not collect an email address, create an account, or subscribe you to a mailing list. Clearing browser data can remove the saved item, and the collection does not sync between devices.

Can I suggest a campus topic?

Yes. Use the Contact page and name the country, institution, language, or situation relevant to your suggestion. Explain what makes the topic interesting and include a direct source where possible. Suggestions help shape development, but they do not guarantee inclusion or establish a release date.

When will Eurollion launch?

There is no announced release date. The current milestone is the introduction page and its artwork. A playable edition will need its own completed implementation and checks before the availability label changes. For now, explore the collection or visit the existing Kegillion development edition.
