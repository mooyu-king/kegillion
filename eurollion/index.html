Discover Eurollion with Kegillion

Play Eurollion, the European campus trivia game in the Kegillion collection. Answer seven prompts about student life and the wider culture around it. Choose Rare or Popular, pick daily seven or practice, and meet Lea in an illustrated university courtyard.

Eurollion has its own question bank and progress, while Kegillion offers the American campus experience. This guide explains the European topics, scoring choices, daily reset, and answer limits. Use the shortcuts to view the artwork, find a rule, or switch campuses without searching through a long menu.

01

Why Eurollion follows the Kegillion name

Eurollion takes its naming cue from Kegillion and brings that campus guessing idea into a European setting. Kegillion is the American campus game in this collection, while Eurollion has its own European questions, characters, and saved rounds.

If you found this page while looking for Kegillion, the American game remains one click away. Stay with Eurollion to try language levels, degree cycles, regional foods, university cities, and other European topics. Familiarity with Kegillion can help you understand the format, but it does not supply the European answers. Read each prompt carefully before choosing a response.

02

Meet Lea on the Eurollion campus

Lea welcomes you to Eurollion beside warm stone arches, flowering plants, and parked bicycles. Her green cardigan, notebook, and canvas bag establish a different scene from the baseball clothing of the Kegillion host. The courtyard is an imagined university setting, not a reconstruction of one institution.

Open the artwork above to inspect the scenery and character details. These Eurollion illustrations show the visual setting rather than a completed score screen. Inside the game, select one of six student characters before starting. Kegillion keeps its own American campus presentation.

03

European questions with a clear scope

Eurollion contains five topic groups: campus life, food and snacks, games and sports, music and media, and schools and world. Each group has three hundred questions. Returning Kegillion players can use the topic selection to try a different kind of knowledge instead of relying on their usual strengths.

One Eurollion prompt asks for one of the three main Bologna higher-education cycles: Bachelor, Master, or Doctorate. Another asks for one of the six main CEFR language levels, from A1 through C2. These bounded questions reward attention to the requested set. Unlike an open request for ideas, a named list has a defined scope; experience with Kegillion should never replace reading that scope.

04

Rare and Popular: choose your strategy

Eurollion offers Rare and Popular modes. Rare rewards accepted answers with lower scores; Popular rewards those with higher scores. Kegillion players will recognise the two contrasting aims. For the Bologna question, the current European bank gives Bachelor a score of one hundred and Doctorate zero. Bachelor therefore helps a Popular attempt, while Doctorate helps a Rare attempt.

Eurollion scores are editorial familiarity estimates, not percentages from a live student survey. As with Kegillion, a high number does not mean a qualification is academically better. Skipping or timing out cannot earn a perfect Rare answer: it is treated as an unsuccessful response. When switching back to Kegillion, check the selected mode again; the same instinct to name an obvious answer can help or hurt depending on your aim.

05

A daily seven or a practice break

Choose daily seven in Eurollion for a timed sequence of seven questions, with twenty-five seconds for each response. Kegillion has a separate daily experience. Completing the European round does not complete the American one, and switching pages does not transfer a score between them.

Practice in Eurollion gives you room to explore a subject without the daily deadline. Leave the practice timer off to think, or enable it for timed answering. If Kegillion is already part of your routine, try a European practice round before adding another daily game. Read the feedback after answering: the explanation and scoring reason are more useful than memorising a number with no context.

06

From shared kitchens to university cities

Eurollion connects campus life with knowledge students might bring to a kitchen, music conversation, or weekend trip. A food prompt asks for an Italian pasta shape suitable for a shared student kitchen; it wants a shape, not a sauce. Another asks for a French bakery sweet. These prompts give Kegillion visitors concrete regional subjects without claiming that every European student eats the same things.

Other Eurollion questions ask for a UK university town or city, or a French city with a university. Open questions can have reasonable answers beyond the recorded examples. The game supports stored aliases, but it cannot recognise every spelling or valid possibility. If an answer is not recognised, check the wording and try another within the remaining time. For feedback about either this game or Kegillion, include the exact prompt and your answer.

07

Keep Eurollion and Kegillion within reach

The heart button saves Eurollion to Favorites in this browser. Save Kegillion too if you enjoy alternating between campuses. Favorites are bookmarks within the site, not subscriptions or account records. Game progress also stays in browser storage, with separate European and American saves. Clearing site data or changing devices can remove access to a saved round; there is no cloud recovery.

Use the game button to return to Eurollion, or open its standalone view when you want more space. The Kegillion shortcut leads directly to the American game, while the sidebar keeps both destinations accessible. To report a problem, use Contact and say whether you were playing Eurollion or Kegillion, which mode you selected, and what happened before the issue.

Questions before you explore

Is Eurollion a European version of Kegillion?

Eurollion takes its name from Kegillion and shares the campus quiz format. It has a separate European question collection and characters. You can enjoy either game independently; completing the American game is not a requirement for starting here.

Do I need to attend a European university?

No. Eurollion includes approachable food, language, sports, and entertainment topics alongside education questions. Kegillion visitors can start with an untimed practice topic. Read the answer explanations to learn unfamiliar terms without treating one difficult round as a test of belonging.

Does a rare answer always win?

Only an accepted answer can help your Eurollion Rare score. Lower is better in Rare; higher is better in Popular. As in Kegillion, choosing the mode changes your goal. A skipped question or timeout is not a clever rare response.

When does the daily game refresh?

Eurollion uses the calendar date in Paris for its daily rounds. Kegillion progress remains separate, so playing one daily game does not overwrite the other game’s saved results.

Are the pictures actual gameplay screenshots?

The Eurollion gallery contains campus and character artwork, not captures of a completed round. Play above to see the question interface and feedback. The Kegillion link leads to its separate American setting; the gallery does not illustrate that game.

Why was my answer not recognised?

Eurollion checks its recorded answers and aliases within the question’s scope. An open question may have other reasonable responses. Check spelling and the requested category, then try another answer. Send the exact prompt through Contact, identifying Eurollion rather than Kegillion.

Can I switch games without losing progress?

Eurollion and Kegillion use separate browser saves. Moving between their pages does not combine results. Keep the same browser and device to revisit stored progress; clearing site data can erase it. Favorites save page shortcuts, not backup copies of game sessions.

Where should I start if I know Kegillion?

Try Eurollion practice with a topic you enjoy and the timer off. Choose the same scoring mode you use in Kegillion to make the first round familiar, then explore the opposite mode when you understand the European answer feedback.
